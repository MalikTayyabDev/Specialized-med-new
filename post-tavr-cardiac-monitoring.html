Why Post-TAVR Rhythm Monitoring Matters After Discharge

Conduction disturbances are a recognized complication of TAVR, and in some patients they evolve or first appear after the patient has gone home. Outpatient mobile cardiac telemetry gives the structural heart team a way to keep selected patients under rhythm surveillance during that post-discharge period.[1]

The transcatheter valve is deployed close to the atrioventricular node and the left bundle branch, so new left bundle branch block, PR or QRS prolongation and atrioventricular block can follow the procedure. Many of these changes are identified in the procedure room or during inpatient telemetry. Others are delayed: published reports describe high-grade AV block and other conduction changes that were first documented days after discharge, sometimes without symptoms the patient recognized.[2][3]

Post-discharge monitoring addresses a different question from the two it is often confused with:

  • Routine inpatient observation covers the procedure and the hospital stay. It ends when the patient is discharged.
  • Immediate pacemaker indications, such as persistent high-grade or complete AV block, are evaluated and managed by the heart team in the hospital. Outpatient monitoring is not a substitute for that decision.
  • Post-discharge surveillance is for patients who are stable enough to go home but have conduction findings the team wants to keep watching, with a defined pathway for acting on what is found.

Not every TAVR patient needs ambulatory MCT. Whether to monitor, with which modality and for how long are decisions for the treating team, based on ECG findings, clinical context and institutional protocol.

Which Post-TAVR Patients May Warrant Outpatient Monitoring?

Outpatient monitoring after TAVR is considered for selected patients whose ECG findings or clinical course leave the heart team concerned about delayed conduction changes or new arrhythmia. It is prescribed by the treating team, not applied to every patient.[1]

Scenarios in which a structural heart or electrophysiology team may choose ambulatory monitoring include:

  • New or evolving left bundle branch block (LBBB) after valve deployment
  • PR or QRS changes compared with the pre-procedure ECG, including progressive prolongation
  • Baseline right bundle branch block (RBBB) when the team has concern about progression
  • Transient conduction changes during or after the procedure that resolved before discharge
  • New atrial fibrillation or atrial flutter identified around the time of the procedure

These are examples of clinician-selected scenarios, not eligibility criteria. Patient selection, monitoring modality and duration remain with the treating team and should follow institutional protocol, current guidance and payer requirements.

24/7 Attended Monitoring, Alerts and Physician-Led Escalation

During a post-TAVR MCT study, Specialized Medical provides attended surveillance around the clock and presents qualifying findings to the recipients the ordering physician has chosen. The physician’s protocol defines the thresholds, the recipients and the notification method; clinical decisions stay with the treating team.

Attended surveillance

ECG-triggered and patient-selected events are transmitted to an attended monitoring team for analysis and documentation while the study is active.

Selectable notification protocols

The ordering physician selects email, text and/or phone notification, the thresholds that qualify and who on the treating service is contacted.

Patient symptom entries

Patients log symptoms digitally. Each entry is time-linked to the ECG so findings can be reviewed as symptomatic or asymptomatic.

Live operational status

Battery, electrode contact, signal quality and connectivity are monitored, and the patient can be contacted when a problem needs to be corrected.

Physician-led escalation

Escalation follows the pathway the heart team has agreed for its program. Specialized Medical presents findings; the treating physician interprets them and decides what happens next.

Connectivity

LIVE ECG transmission and operational visibility depend on cellular connectivity at the patient’s location. Notification timing depends on the prescribed protocol, signal quality and connectivity, so delivery within a fixed time cannot be guaranteed.

Not an emergency service

MCT is outpatient diagnostic monitoring. It does not replace inpatient observation, a pacemaker when indicated or immediate medical evaluation. Patients with chest pain, fainting, severe shortness of breath or other urgent symptoms should call 911 rather than wait for a monitoring call.

The S-Patch Monitor and the Patient Experience

Post-TAVR studies use the S-Patch cardiac monitoring system, a lightweight wearable monitor that patients wear at home during normal daily activities.

  • Weight: approximately 0.6 oz
  • Water resistance: IP55, for normal daily activities when patients follow the wear instructions
  • Battery: 10-day minimum battery duration
  • Study length: prescribed studies of up to 30 days, with battery management through scheduled patch replacement and/or recharge steps when the study runs longer than one battery window
  • Connectivity: multi-carrier cellular pathways when available; uninterrupted coverage is not guaranteed

At hookup, patients are shown how to wear the monitor, how to log symptoms and what to do if the monitor needs attention. Specialized Medical provides patient education and technical support throughout the study, and can contact the patient when a connection or signal problem is detected.

Clinical Guidance and Research on Post-TAVR Monitoring

Expert consensus supports outpatient ambulatory monitoring for selected patients with conduction changes after TAVR, and observational experience with MCT in this setting has been published. The summaries below are written for clinicians and link to their sources.

ACC Expert Consensus Decision Pathway, 2020 [1]

Describes a risk-based approach to conduction disturbances around TAVR: pre-procedure assessment, monitoring until conduction is stable, and outpatient ambulatory monitoring for at least 14 days in specified patients with new or progressive conduction changes.

Guidance · American College of Cardiology

Outpatient post-TAVR MCT program, 2024 [2]

An observational report of a structured outpatient telemetry program after TAVR. The program recorded rhythm abnormalities after discharge, including conduction abnormalities that led to pacemaker placement and new atrial fibrillation or flutter.

Observational study · PMID 38388248

Post-TAVI conduction systematic review, 2026 [3]

Reviews published studies of conduction disturbances after TAVI. Patient risk varies between studies and populations, and the optimal duration of outpatient monitoring remains uncertain.

Systematic review · Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine

Limits of the evidence

The studies summarized here are largely observational and used their own patient populations, devices and protocols. Their findings are not performance results for Specialized Medical and do not show that monitoring changes clinical outcomes. Specialized Medical records and presents ECG findings for physician review; it does not diagnose or treat, and it cannot ensure that every event is detected.

24–48 Hour Holter Monitoring

A 24–48-hour Holter monitor continuously records the patient’s electrocardiogram (ECG) over one to two days. With LIVE TEST STATUS VISIBILITY on connected studies, Specialized Medical can spot and help resolve technical issues while the patient wears the monitor. Clinical findings are presented in the final report after the study and analysis are complete.

How LIVE TEST STATUS VISIBILITY Helps Keep a Holter Study on Track

During a connected Holter study, Specialized Medical can see whether the battery, electrodes and connection are working as expected and whether ECG data is being received. If the team identifies an issue, it can contact the patient and help correct it during wear. Addressing these issues while the study is active helps preserve usable ECG data and improves the likelihood of completing the prescribed recording. Clinical findings are presented to the ordering clinician in the final report after the study and analysis are complete.

When a Short Holter Study May Help

A clinician may order short-duration Holter monitoring when symptoms such as frequent palpitations are expected within the recording window. The recording can help the clinician compare reported symptom times with the heart’s rhythm and assess rhythm patterns over the prescribed period. The clinician selects the test based on the history, symptoms and question the study needs to answer.[1]

What Happens During the First 48 Hours?

The practice enrolls the patient, applies the wearable and explains how to record symptoms and whom to call for help. For a LIVE STREAMING study, the S-Patch connects by Bluetooth to an assigned phone, which sends ECG data when cellular connectivity is available. Specialized Medical can check indicators such as battery status, electrode contact and whether ECG data is being received. If the team detects a technical issue, it can contact the patient to help resolve it while there is still time to preserve part of the short recording window.

  1. Enroll and prescribeThe practice enrolls the patient and confirms the prescribed recording window.
  2. Apply and instructThe S-Patch is applied, and the patient learns how to log symptoms and whom to call.
  3. Wear with LIVE TEST STATUS VISIBILITYOn connected studies, Specialized Medical follows battery, electrode contact, connectivity and data receipt.
  4. Final reportAfter the study and analysis are complete, the ordering clinician receives the final report.

Equipment details and wear instructions are on the S-Patch cardiac monitoring system and cardiac monitoring equipment pages.

Urgent symptoms

For urgent symptoms such as chest pain, severe shortness of breath or stroke symptoms, call 911. For device or setup questions, use the support number supplied with your study.

What the Clinician Gets After the Study

The final report summarizes available ECG findings for the ordering clinician. It may include heart-rate and rhythm information, ectopy, representative ECG strips and any available symptom-to-rhythm correlations. The clinician reviews the report alongside the patient’s history and other findings.

Questions About 24–48 Hour Holter Monitoring

A study lasting exactly 48 hours falls within the short Holter duration band. The first extended continuous ECG coding band begins after 48 hours.

A clinician may select it when symptoms occur frequently or can be reliably reproduced within a day or two. The clinician chooses the monitoring window based on the individual evaluation.

Cardiac Event Monitoring for Physician Practices

Cardiac event monitoring captures ECG information associated with intermittent symptoms or selected automatically detected rhythm events over a prescribed period of up to 30 days. It helps the ordering clinician evaluate symptoms that may not occur during a short Holter study.[1][2]

Specialized Medical combines connected monitoring, a simple patient symptom logger, LIVE technical-status visibility and patient support. Qualifying findings are presented during the study according to the ordering physician’s notification protocol, followed by a physician-ready final report.

When May a Physician Choose Event Monitoring?

Event monitoring may help when palpitations, dizziness or other episodes suggest a possible rhythm problem but are not expected to occur during a one- or two-day recording. The physician considers symptom frequency, the clinical question and the patient’s ability to use the prescribed system.[1][2]

The monitoring period is selected for the individual patient. Up to 30 days creates a longer opportunity to capture relevant events; it is not a requirement that every patient complete 30 days.

  • Intermittent symptoms that may fall outside a 24–48-hour Holter window.
  • A need to compare reported symptom times with available ECG information.
  • Selected automatically detected events when that capability is enabled in the prescribed configuration.

How Event Monitoring Works – and How It Differs

The patient wears the prescribed monitor and uses the assigned phone to record symptoms. Patient-marked events and selected automatically detected events, when enabled, enter the Event Monitoring review and communication workflow.

  1. PrescribePrescribe the study and agree on notification criteria.
  2. Set upSet up the monitor and assigned phone; teach symptom logging.
  3. Capture and reviewCapture and review events under the prescribed workflow.
  4. Findings and final reportPresent qualifying findings during the study and deliver the final report.

Event Monitoring focuses on event capture and symptom correlation. Holter and Long-Term Holter provide continuous recording with clinical findings presented after the final report. MCT adds a distinct service involving concurrent computerized rhythm analysis and attended surveillance. The ordering physician selects the service that fits the clinical need.[1]

Using a connected monitor does not make every test MCT. The prescribed service determines which findings are reviewed, how they are presented and when the practice is contacted. All prescribed services are described on Cardiac Monitoring Services.

Patient-Marked Symptoms and Automatic Events

Patient-triggered entries add the patient’s experience to the ECG record. The patient uses Log Symptoms on the assigned phone, following the study instructions. The recorded time helps the clinician compare symptoms with the available ECG.

Automatic detection, when enabled, can capture selected rhythm events without a patient entry. It complements symptom logging; it does not capture every possible event or replace the patient’s instructions.[1]

Symptoms do not always correspond to an arrhythmia. A symptom entry alongside the recorded rhythm can still help the physician evaluate the episode.[1]

LIVE Test-Status Visibility and Help During Wear

Specialized Medical can follow the technical condition of a connected study: battery status, electrode contact, signal quality, Bluetooth communication, cellular connectivity and whether ECG data is being received. When an issue is identified, our team can contact the patient to help restore the study.

The S-Patch cardiac monitoring system communicates with the assigned phone by Bluetooth. The phone transmits through available cellular service, using the program’s multi-carrier connectivity approach. Live visibility and transmission depend on connectivity at the patient’s location.

Findings During the Study. A Clear Final Report.

Qualifying Event Monitoring findings are presented while the study is active according to the ordering physician’s notification protocol. The protocol sets the criteria, recipients and communication methods, which may include email, text and phone. Routine event documentation does not necessarily trigger a clinical notification.

Digital symptoms appear with the corresponding ECG information so the physician can see the reported symptom context. The final report organizes captured findings, representative ECG strips and available symptom correlations for review, interpretation and signature.

Delivery timing depends on the protocol, signal quality and connectivity. The treating clinician interprets the findings and determines the next step.
